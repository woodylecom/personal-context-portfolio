# Voice: Anti-Examples

## What This File Is For

Paired with [`communication-style.md`](communication-style.md). That file describes your voice in the abstract; this one shows it concretely — good vs. bad, side by side. When an agent is producing content on your behalf (emails, messages, drafts) or replying to you, these pairs are the calibration set. Abstract descriptions ("I'm direct and concise") don't transfer into an agent's actual output nearly as well as seeing the exact sentence it should never write.

This is one of five **optional modules** in this kit (see [`unknowns.md`](unknowns.md), [`operational-boundaries.md`](operational-boundaries.md), [`current-state.md`](current-state.md), [`personal-use.md`](personal-use.md)). The core ten files are a complete portfolio on their own — this adds more precision once you want it.

---

## Interview Protocol

*Hand this entire file to your AI build partner and say "let's do this one." Best done right after `communication-style.md`, while that interview is still fresh.*

**Instructions for the build partner:** For each category below, ask the user for a real example of something that sounds wrong to them, then ask what they'd actually say instead. Push for their literal wording, not a paraphrase — the exact sentence is the point. If they're stuck, offer a common AI-generated phrase as a prompt ("would you ever say 'I hope this message finds you well'?") and use their reaction to calibrate.

**Questions to ask:**

1. Think of a time an AI wrote something "for" you that made you cringe. What did it actually say?
2. What would you have said instead, in that exact situation?
3. Are there specific words or phrases that make you distrust a piece of writing immediately — corporate-speak, AI tells, anything?
4. Does your tone shift by audience — how you'd write to your manager vs. your team vs. an external contact? Give me a real example of each if so.
5. Is there a phrase that, if an AI used it, would tell you it clearly doesn't know you? ("I'm here to help!", "Certainly!", etc. — but get their own list.)

**When you have enough:** After 4-6 real do/don't pairs across at least two categories (openers, framing/hedging, corporate tells). More pairs make this file more useful — it's one of the few files where longer is fine.

**After drafting:** Present the draft and ask if any of the "good" examples still sound slightly off. This file rewards a second pass more than most — the goal is your exact voice, not a plausible one.
